Small business websites are hacked every day, and almost never because someone chose them specifically. Automated tools scan millions of sites looking for an out-of-date plugin, a weak password or a forgotten admin account. If your site has one, it will eventually be found. Here is how to make sure there is nothing for them to find.

Why anyone would hack a small website

It is rarely personal. A hacked site can be used to send spam, host scam pages, redirect visitors to dodgy shops, or quietly add hidden links that boost someone else's search rankings. You may not notice for weeks, but Google often does, and it can flag your site as dangerous or drop it from search results altogether.

1. Keep everything updated

Most WordPress hacks come through plugins and themes with known security holes that already have a fix available. Updates exist because problems have been found and published, which means attackers know about them too. Update WordPress, every plugin and your theme promptly, and keep the server's PHP version supported.

2. Remove what you do not use

Every plugin is extra code that needs looking after. A plugin that has been switched off but left installed can still be attacked. If you are not using it, delete it, and be wary of plugins that have not been updated by their developer for a year or more.

3. Strong passwords and two-step sign-in

  • Give every person their own login, never a shared one
  • Use long, unique passwords, ideally from a password manager
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for every administrator account
  • Remove logins for people who no longer work with you
  • Only give administrator access to people who really need it

4. Backups you can actually restore

A backup is your safety net if everything else fails. Good backups run automatically every day, are stored away from the website itself, are kept for long enough to go back before a problem started, and have been tested. A backup nobody has ever restored is a hope rather than a plan.

5. A firewall and malware scanning

A web application firewall sits in front of your site and blocks known attacks, bad bots and repeated login attempts before they reach WordPress. Regular malware scanning checks your files for anything that should not be there, so a problem is caught in hours rather than months.

6. SSL on every page

Your site should load securely over HTTPS everywhere, with the padlock showing on every page. It protects anything visitors type into forms, browsers warn people away from sites without it, and Google prefers secure sites in its results.

Signs your site may already be hacked

  • Visitors are sent to other websites, especially on mobile
  • Google shows strange titles or foreign-language pages for your site
  • Your browser or Google warns that the site is dangerous
  • You see admin users you do not recognise
  • Your host tells you the site is sending spam or using too many resources

If any of these sound familiar, act quickly. Change your passwords, contact whoever looks after the site, and do not simply delete the odd files you can see, as hacks usually leave back doors behind.
