Two small renewals keep your business online: your domain name and your SSL certificate. Miss either one and the effect is immediate. Your website disappears or shows a security warning, and with a lapsed domain your email can stop too. Here is how to make sure it never happens to you.

What happens when a domain expires

Your domain is rented, usually a year at a time. If the renewal fails, often because a card on file has expired or a reminder went to an old email address, your website and email stop working. There is normally a short grace period to renew, but after that the domain can be released, and some people buy up lapsed domains to resell them at a high price.

How to protect your domain

  • Turn on auto-renew, and keep the payment card up to date
  • Make sure renewal reminders go to an inbox someone actually reads, not a former employee's address
  • Register the domain in the business's name, and keep the login details somewhere safe
  • Consider renewing for several years at a time

What an SSL certificate does

An SSL certificate is what puts the padlock next to your web address and encrypts information moving between your site and its visitors, such as contact form details. When it expires, browsers show a full-page warning that your site is not secure, and most visitors leave immediately.

Certificates are getting shorter

The industry is steadily reducing how long certificates last, so they have to be renewed more often. Certificates used to last over a year; they are now limited to around six months, and that will keep falling over the next few years. Renewing by hand is no longer realistic. Certificates should renew automatically, and something should check that they actually did.
