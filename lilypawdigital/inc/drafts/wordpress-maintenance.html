A WordPress website is not something you build once and forget. Behind the scenes it is made of WordPress itself, a theme and a collection of plugins, and all of them are updated regularly. Leave them alone for long enough and problems start to appear. Here are the signs your site needs some attention.

1. You see "updates available" every time you log in

Many updates fix security holes. Out-of-date plugins are one of the most common ways WordPress sites get hacked, so a long list of waiting updates is a risk, not just an inconvenience.

2. You are not sure when it was last backed up

A backup is only useful if it exists, is recent and can actually be restored. If you do not know where your backups are kept or when the last one ran, assume you do not have one.

3. It has slowed down

Sites tend to get slower over time as plugins pile up and old ones stop being looked after. Slow pages frustrate visitors and can count against you in search results.

4. Something has quietly stopped working

A contact form that no longer sends, a gallery that will not load, a layout broken on phones. These problems are easy to miss if nobody checks, and every missed enquiry is a lost customer.

5. Your browser shows a security warning

If your SSL certificate lapses, browsers warn visitors that your site is not secure, and most will leave immediately. Certificates now need renewing more often than they used to, so this is worth monitoring.

What good maintenance looks like

  • Regular updates to WordPress, plugins and the theme, checked afterwards so nothing breaks
  • Automatic backups, stored away from the website, and checked
  • Security checks and monitoring
  • Someone to call when something goes wrong
