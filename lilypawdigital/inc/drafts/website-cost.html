The three main routes

  • Do it yourself with a website builder. The cheapest way to start, usually a monthly subscription. You save money but spend your own time, and the result depends on your design skills and patience.
  • A freelancer or small studio. Usually a few hundred to a couple of thousand pounds for a small business site. You get a professional result and someone to call, at a sensible price.
  • A larger agency. Often several thousand pounds or more. Worth it for complex projects, but more than most small businesses need.

What makes a website cost more

  • Number of pages. A single page is quicker to build than fifteen.
  • Custom design. A design made for your business takes longer than adapting a template.
  • Online shop. Selling online means products, payments, delivery options and stock, which adds work.
  • Features. Booking systems, member areas and integrations with other software all add time.
  • Writing and photos. If you need the words written or photos taken for you, that is extra.

The costs after launch

The build is not the only cost. Every website has a few running costs, and it is worth knowing about them before you start:

  • Domain name. Your web address, renewed every year.
  • Hosting. Where the website lives, usually paid monthly.
  • Email. Addresses on your own domain, sometimes included with hosting.
  • Maintenance. Updates, backups and security checks, especially for WordPress sites.
  • Changes. New pages, updated prices and seasonal offers over time.

A cheap build with expensive running costs is not a bargain, so always ask what you will pay each year, not just up front.

Questions to ask any web designer

  • What is the total price, and what does it include?
  • What will I pay every month or year after launch?
  • Who owns the website and the domain?
  • Will it work properly on phones?
  • What happens if I need changes later?
