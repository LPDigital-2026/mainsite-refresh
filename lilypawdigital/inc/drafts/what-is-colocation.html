If your business runs its own servers, they probably live in a cupboard, a back office or under someone's desk. Colocation means moving that equipment into a professional data centre, where it gets proper power, cooling, security and connectivity, while you keep full ownership and control of it.

How colocation works

You rent space in a rack, from a single unit for one server up to a full rack, and bring your own hardware. The data centre provides the building, the power, the cooling and the network connection. Your servers, your software and your data stay exactly as they are; they just live somewhere far better suited to them.

Colocation, cloud or the office?

  • In the office: cheap to start, but exposed to power cuts, overheating, break-ins and slow office broadband.
  • In the cloud: no hardware to own, but monthly costs can climb quickly for steady, always-on workloads, and you are tied to one provider.
  • Colocation: you keep your own hardware and costs stay predictable, with data-centre reliability.

What you get in a data centre

  • Backup power: UPS systems and a standby generator that take over if the mains fails
  • Precision cooling, so your equipment runs at the right temperature all year
  • Fire suppression designed for IT equipment, using gas rather than water
  • Physical security: fencing, CCTV, alarms and controlled access
  • Fast, resilient connectivity from multiple fibre providers

Who it suits

Colocation is a good fit if you run your own servers for websites, business applications, backups, private cloud or game servers, and you want them to be more reliable without handing everything over to a cloud provider. It is also a sensible home for a second copy of your data, away from the office, in case of fire, flood or theft.
