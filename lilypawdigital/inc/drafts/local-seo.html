When someone in Gateshead searches for a plumber, an accountant or a hairdresser, Google does not show them the best business in the country. It shows them the best nearby. That is local SEO, and for most small businesses it matters far more than ranking nationally. The businesses that appear at the top are rarely the biggest. They are the ones that have given Google clear, consistent information. Here is how to do the same.

How local search works

For searches with local intent, such as "electrician near me" or "cafe in Hexham", Google usually shows a map with three businesses underneath, followed by the normal results. To decide who appears, it looks at three things: how relevant your business is to the search, how close you are to the person searching, and how well known and trusted your business seems.

1. Claim your Google Business Profile

This is the single most important step, and it is free. Your Business Profile is what appears in the map results and on the right-hand side when someone searches for your name. Fill in every section properly:

  • Choose the most accurate main category, and add secondary ones
  • Add your opening hours, and keep holiday hours up to date
  • Write a clear description of what you do and the areas you cover
  • Add real photos of your premises, your team and your work
  • List your services, with prices where it makes sense

If you serve customers at their location rather than at a shop, you can set a service area covering the towns you work in instead of showing your address.

2. Keep your details identical everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number should be written exactly the same on your website, your Business Profile, Facebook, directories and anywhere else you are listed. Small differences, such as an old address or a different phone number, make Google less sure which details are right.

3. Ask for reviews, and reply to them

Reviews influence both your ranking and whether people choose you. The easiest way to get more is simply to ask happy customers, with a direct link to your review page. Reply to every review, good or bad, politely and promptly. A calm, helpful reply to a poor review often impresses future customers more than a page of five stars.

4. Make your website say where you work

Google needs to see the places you serve on your own website, written naturally. Mention your town and the areas you cover on your home page and service pages. If you work across the region, a page for each service or area, with genuinely useful content about it, does far more than a list of place names at the bottom of the page.

5. Make it fast and work properly on phones

Most local searches happen on a phone, often when someone needs something soon. If your site is slow, hard to read on a small screen, or hides your phone number, people will go back and pick the next business. Put your phone number and address where they are easy to find, and make the number tap to call.

6. Get mentioned locally

Links and mentions from other local websites tell Google you are an established part of the community. Think local business groups, chambers of commerce, sponsorships of local clubs and events, suppliers you work with, and local news.

A checklist to start with

  • Claim and complete your Google Business Profile
  • Check your name, address and phone number match everywhere
  • Send your last ten happy customers a link asking for a review
  • Make sure your home page says where you are and who you serve
  • Test your website on your own phone
