Plenty of small businesses get by on a Facebook page and word of mouth. So is a website still worth having in 2026? For most local businesses, yes, and not for the reasons you might think.

People check before they call

Even when someone hears about you from a friend, they will usually look you up before getting in touch. If they find a clear, up-to-date website with your services, prices and contact details, you look established and trustworthy. If they find nothing, or a page that has not been touched in years, many will quietly move on to the next name.

You own it

Social media platforms change the rules regularly. Posts reach fewer people, accounts get locked, and you cannot control how your business is presented. A website is yours: your name, your layout, your message, and it stays put.

It helps you get found locally

When someone searches for a service "near me" or in Newcastle, Gateshead or Sunderland, Google looks at your website alongside your Google Business Profile. A site that clearly says what you do and where you work gives you a much better chance of appearing in those local results.

It answers questions for you

Opening hours, prices, what is included, where to park: a good website answers the questions you get asked every day, so fewer calls are spent on the basics and more on real enquiries.

What a small business website needs

  • A clear headline saying what you do and where
  • Your services, ideally with prices or a starting price
  • Reviews or examples of your work
  • An easy way to get in touch: phone, email and a short form
  • It must work well on a phone, because that is where most people will see it

That does not have to mean a big project. A single well-made page is enough for many businesses, and you can add more as you grow.
