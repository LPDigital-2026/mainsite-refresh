If your emails keep landing in spam, or a customer says they never received your invoice, the cause is very often three missing records in your domain's DNS: SPF, DKIM and DMARC.

They sound technical, but each one has a simple job. Together they prove to other email servers that a message really came from you.

What SPF does

SPF lists the servers that are allowed to send email for your domain. When a message arrives, the receiving server checks it came from one of them.

  • One SPF record per domain
  • List every service that sends as you, such as Microsoft 365 and your website
  • Finish with ~all or -all

What DKIM does

DKIM adds a digital signature to every email. The receiving server checks the signature against a public key published in your DNS.

Set up all three and your emails are far less likely to be marked as spam, and far harder for anyone to fake.

What DMARC does

DMARC tells receiving servers what to do when a message fails the SPF and DKIM checks, and sends you reports so you can see who is sending as you.

v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:reports@example.co.uk

Where to start

Start DMARC in monitoring mode, read the reports for a couple of weeks, then tighten it. If you would rather not touch DNS yourself, we can set it up for you.
